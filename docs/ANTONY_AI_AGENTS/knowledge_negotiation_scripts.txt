SUBSCRIPTION NEGOTIATION SCRIPT KNOWLEDGE BASE
==============================================

DOCUMENT PURPOSE
This knowledge base provides comprehensive guidance for generating effective negotiation scripts for subscription retention, including opening strategies, objection handling, and fallback positions.

==============================================
SECTION 1: NEGOTIATION FUNDAMENTALS
==============================================

1.1 CORE PRINCIPLES
- Loyalty emphasis: Always lead with subscription duration and loyalty
- Empathy + firmness: Be understanding but clear about needs
- Multiple options: Provide 3 distinct fallback positions
- Specific numbers: Use exact costs, dates, and percentages
- Natural flow: Write conversational, not scripted-sounding language

1.2 PSYCHOLOGY OF RETENTION
- Retention departments have more authority than first-line support
- Long-term customers are more valuable than new customers
- Cancellation threats are taken seriously
- Competitors' pricing is powerful leverage
- Hardship framing triggers retention protocols

1.3 TIMING STRATEGIES
- Call during off-peak hours (mid-morning, mid-afternoon)
- Avoid Mondays and Fridays
- Be prepared to spend 15-30 minutes on the call
- Have account information ready
- Document the call (date, time, representative name)

SECTION 2: SCRIPT STRUCTURE
==============================================

2.1 REQUIRED SECTIONS (IN ORDER)
1. [YOUR OPENING] - Loyalty-focused introduction
2. [MAIN REQUEST] - Primary ask (return to original pricing or discount)
3. [IF THEY SAY NO] - Empathetic but firm response
4. [FALLBACK POSITION 1] - Partial discount for limited time
5. [FALLBACK POSITION 2] - Downgrade or pause option
6. [FALLBACK POSITION 3] - Competitor price matching
7. [OBJECTION HANDLING] - Responses to common objections
8. [CLOSING] - Clear next steps and timeline

2.2 OPENING STATEMENT FORMULA
Components:
- Greeting and account identification
- Loyalty statement (mention duration)
- Problem statement (price increase or hardship)
- Value statement (what you appreciate about the service)
- Transition to request

Example:
"Hi, I'm calling about my [Provider] account. I've been a loyal subscriber for [X] months, and I noticed my bill increased from $[Original] to $[Current]. I really value [Provider] and the service has been great, but this [X]% increase is difficult for me right now given [hardship type]."

2.3 MAIN REQUEST FORMULA
Components:
- Clear ask (specific pricing or discount)
- Justification (loyalty, long-term value)
- Collaborative framing ("find a solution together")

Example:
"I'd like to request that my account be returned to the original $[Original] pricing, or receive a loyalty discount that brings me closer to that amount. As a long-term customer of [X] months, I'm hoping we can find a solution that works for both of us."

==============================================
SECTION 3: FALLBACK POSITIONS
==============================================

3.1 FALLBACK POSITION 1: TEMPORARY DISCOUNT
Strategy: Request 50% off for 6 months
Rationale: Gives time to adjust budget, shows willingness to compromise
Success rate: High (common retention offer)

Script:
"Would you be able to offer me 50% off for the next 6 months while I evaluate whether I can continue the subscription at the full price? That would give me time to adjust my budget and see if I can make it work long-term."

Variations:
- 3 months at 50% off
- 6 months at 30% off
- First month free, then reduced rate

3.2 FALLBACK POSITION 2: DOWNGRADE OR PAUSE
Strategy: Request lower-tier plan or temporary pause
Rationale: Shows you want to stay but need flexibility
Success rate: Medium to High

Script:
"Is there a temporary pause option or a lower-tier plan I could switch to? I'd rather downgrade than cancel completely. What options do you have for customers who want to stay but need to reduce costs?"

Variations:
- Seasonal pause (3-6 months)
- Switch to basic/standard tier
- Pay-per-use option if available

3.3 FALLBACK POSITION 3: COMPETITOR MATCHING
Strategy: Reference competitor pricing and request price match
Rationale: Shows you've researched alternatives, creates urgency
Success rate: Medium (depends on competitive landscape)

Script:
"I've been looking at [Competitor] and they're offering similar service for $[Lower Price]. Would [Provider] be willing to match or beat that price to keep me as a customer? I'd prefer to stay with [Provider] since I'm familiar with the service, but the price difference is significant."

Competitor Examples:
- Streaming: Netflix vs Disney+ vs Hulu vs HBO Max
- Music: Spotify vs Apple Music vs YouTube Music
- Software: Adobe vs Affinity vs Canva
- Fitness: Peloton vs Apple Fitness+ vs Beachbody

==============================================
SECTION 4: OBJECTION HANDLING
==============================================

4.1 OBJECTION: "That's our best price"
Response Strategy: Question authority, request escalation

Script:
"I appreciate that, but I've been a customer for [X] months and I'm hoping my loyalty counts for something. Is there a retention department or supervisor who might have more flexibility to work with long-term customers like me?"

Follow-up if still no:
"I understand you may not have the authority to offer discounts. Could you transfer me to someone who does? I'd really like to explore all options before I have to cancel."

4.2 OBJECTION: "We can't change pricing"
Response Strategy: Challenge the policy, reference retention offers

Script:
"I understand that's the policy, but I've heard from other customers that retention offers are available for long-term subscribers. Can you check if there are any loyalty programs or retention offers that might apply to my account?"

Follow-up:
"If there's truly no flexibility on pricing, can you help me understand what options I have? I don't want to cancel, but I need to make this work financially."

4.3 OBJECTION: "You can cancel anytime"
Response Strategy: Express preference to stay, create urgency

Script:
"I'd prefer not to cancel - I enjoy [Provider] and I've been happy with the service for [X] months. But if there's truly no flexibility on pricing, I'll need to make that difficult decision. Can you confirm there are no retention offers available before I proceed with cancellation?"

Follow-up:
"Before I cancel, can you transfer me to the retention or loyalty department? I want to make sure I've explored every option to stay with [Provider]."

4.4 OBJECTION: "The price increase is company-wide"
Response Strategy: Acknowledge but emphasize individual loyalty

Script:
"I understand it's a company-wide increase, but I'm asking for an individual loyalty discount based on my [X] months as a customer. Many companies offer retention discounts to long-term customers even when there are general price increases. Is that something [Provider] can do?"

4.5 OBJECTION: "We'll lose money if we discount"
Response Strategy: Frame long-term value vs. churn cost

Script:
"I understand the concern, but losing me as a customer after [X] months means losing all future revenue from my account. A temporary discount or reduced rate would keep me as a paying customer rather than losing me entirely. What's the cost of acquiring a new customer to replace me?"

SECTION 5: PROVIDER-SPECIFIC STRATEGIES
==============================================

5.1 STREAMING SERVICES

NETFLIX
- Retention strategy: Moderate flexibility
- Best approach: Emphasize long-term loyalty, mention competitor pricing
- Common offers: 1-3 months at reduced rate
- Key phrase: "I've been with Netflix since [year]"

SPOTIFY
- Retention strategy: Limited flexibility on individual plans
- Best approach: Ask about family plan conversion or student discount
- Common offers: 3 months at $0.99, family plan upgrade
- Key phrase: "I'm considering Apple Music's family plan"

DISNEY+
- Retention strategy: Bundle-focused
- Best approach: Ask about Disney Bundle (Disney+, Hulu, ESPN+)
- Common offers: Bundle discount, annual plan discount
- Key phrase: "The bundle seems like better value"

HULU
- Retention strategy: Moderate to high flexibility
- Best approach: Mention ad-free tier cost, competitor pricing
- Common offers: Reduced rate for 6-12 months
- Key phrase: "I'm comparing with Netflix and Disney+"

5.2 SOFTWARE SUBSCRIPTIONS

ADOBE CREATIVE CLOUD
- Retention strategy: High flexibility for annual plans
- Best approach: Mention Affinity or Canva as alternatives
- Common offers: 25-40% off for 12 months
- Key phrase: "Affinity is a one-time purchase"

MICROSOFT 365
- Retention strategy: Moderate flexibility
- Best approach: Mention Google Workspace or free alternatives
- Common offers: Family plan upgrade, annual discount
- Key phrase: "Google Workspace is cheaper for my needs"

5.3 FITNESS SUBSCRIPTIONS

PELOTON
- Retention strategy: High flexibility (high churn concern)
- Best approach: Mention Apple Fitness+ or cheaper alternatives
- Common offers: 50% off for 3-6 months, pause option
- Key phrase: "Apple Fitness+ is $10/month"

PLANET FITNESS
- Retention strategy: Low flexibility (franchise model)
- Best approach: Request freeze instead of cancellation
- Common offers: Freeze for 1-3 months
- Key phrase: "I'd like to freeze rather than cancel"

SECTION 6: HARDSHIP TYPES & FRAMING
==============================================

6.1 FINANCIAL HARDSHIP
Framing: "Budget constraints due to [reason]"
Effective phrases:
- "I'm on a tight budget right now"
- "I need to reduce my monthly expenses"
- "I'm prioritizing essential expenses"

Script addition:
"I'm dealing with some financial constraints right now and need to reduce my monthly expenses. I value [Provider], but I need to make some tough decisions about what I can afford."

6.2 REDUCED USAGE
Framing: "Not using the service as much as before"
Effective phrases:
- "My usage has decreased significantly"
- "I'm not getting the same value as before"
- "I'm only using it occasionally now"

Script addition:
"I've noticed my usage has decreased significantly over the past few months. I'm only using [Provider] occasionally now, and it's hard to justify the full price for limited use."

6.3 COMPETITOR OFFER
Framing: "Found better value elsewhere"
Effective phrases:
- "I've found a better deal with [Competitor]"
- "Competitors are offering more for less"
- "I'm comparing options and [Competitor] looks attractive"

Script addition:
"I've been comparing options and [Competitor] is offering [specific feature/price]. I'd prefer to stay with [Provider] since I'm familiar with it, but the value proposition is hard to ignore."

6.4 PRICE INCREASE
Framing: "Recent price increase is unacceptable"
Effective phrases:
- "The [X]% increase is too much"
- "I didn't budget for this increase"
- "The new price doesn't match the value"

Script addition:
"The recent price increase from $[Original] to $[Current] - a [X]% jump - is more than I budgeted for. I understand costs go up, but this increase is significant and I need to reconsider whether I can continue."

SECTION 7: ADVANCED TECHNIQUES
==============================================

7.1 THE PAUSE TECHNIQUE
When to use: When you want to show you're thinking seriously
How: After their response, pause for 2-3 seconds before responding
Effect: Creates pressure, shows you're considering cancellation

Example:
Rep: "I'm sorry, that's our best price."
You: [Pause 2-3 seconds] "I see. That's disappointing. Let me think about whether I can make that work..."

7.2 THE COMPARISON TECHNIQUE
When to use: When you have specific competitor pricing
How: Present exact comparison with features and pricing
Effect: Shows you've done research, creates competitive pressure

Example:
"I've done a detailed comparison. [Competitor] offers [features] for $[price], while [Provider] is now $[price] for [features]. That's a $[difference] monthly difference, or $[annual] per year. Can [Provider] match that?"

7.3 THE LOYALTY CALCULATION
When to use: For long-term subscriptions (12+ months)
How: Calculate total amount paid over subscription period
Effect: Emphasizes your value as a customer

Example:
"I've been a subscriber for [X] months, which means I've paid [Provider] approximately $[total] over that time. As a customer who's contributed $[total] to your revenue, I'm hoping there's some flexibility on pricing."

7.4 THE BUNDLE INQUIRY
When to use: When provider offers multiple services
How: Ask about bundle discounts or package deals
Effect: May unlock better pricing through bundling

Example:
"I'm currently subscribed to [Service A]. Do you have any bundle deals if I add [Service B]? Sometimes the bundle is cheaper than individual services."

SECTION 8: TIMING & FOLLOW-UP
==============================================

8.1 BEST TIMES TO CALL
Optimal:
- Tuesday-Thursday, 10am-11am or 2pm-3pm (local time)
- Mid-month (avoid billing cycle dates)
- After price increase announcement (retention offers active)

Avoid:
- Mondays (high call volume)
- Fridays (staff may be less empowered)
- First/last day of month (billing issues)
- Holidays or holiday weeks

8.2 CALL DURATION EXPECTATIONS
- Initial conversation: 10-15 minutes
- If transferred to retention: Add 10-15 minutes
- Total time budget: 30-45 minutes maximum

8.3 FOLLOW-UP STRATEGY
If they say "We'll call you back":
- Get specific timeframe (24-48 hours)
- Get reference number
- Get direct callback number
- Confirm what they're checking on

If no callback:
- Call back after promised timeframe
- Reference previous call and representative name
- Ask to speak to retention department directly

8.4 DOCUMENTATION
Record during/after call:
- Date and time of call
- Representative name and ID
- Offers presented
- Promises made
- Reference numbers
- Next steps agreed upon

==============================================
SECTION 9: SUCCESS METRICS & EXPECTATIONS
==============================================

9.1 REALISTIC SUCCESS RATES
- Temporary discount (3-6 months): 60-70% success rate
- Permanent discount: 20-30% success rate
- Downgrade option: 70-80% success rate
- Price match: 30-40% success rate

9.2 TYPICAL RETENTION OFFERS
Common offers by provider type:
- Streaming: 1-3 months at 50% off
- Software: 25-40% off for 12 months
- Fitness: 50% off for 3-6 months or freeze option
- News/Media: 3-6 months at promotional rate

9.3 WHEN TO ACCEPT AN OFFER
Accept if:
- Discount brings price to acceptable level
- Temporary discount gives time to evaluate
- Downgrade meets your actual usage needs
- Offer is better than canceling and resubscribing

Consider declining if:
- Discount is minimal (less than 20%)
- Terms are too restrictive
- You genuinely don't use the service
- Competitor offers significantly better value

SECTION 10: EXAMPLE SCRIPTS
==============================================

10.1 COMPLETE SCRIPT: PRICE INCREASE SCENARIO

[YOUR OPENING]
"Hi, I'm calling about my Netflix account. I've been a loyal subscriber for 24 months, and I noticed my bill increased from $12.99 to $15.99. I really value Netflix and have enjoyed the service, but this 23% increase is difficult for me right now given my current budget constraints."

[MAIN REQUEST]
"I'd like to request that my account be returned to the original $12.99 pricing, or receive a loyalty discount that brings me closer to that amount. As a long-term customer of 24 months, I'm hoping we can find a solution that works for both of us."

[IF THEY SAY NO]
"I understand that's the standard pricing, but given my 24 months of loyalty and the fact that I'm considering canceling due to budget constraints, is there any flexibility you can offer? I'd really like to stay with Netflix if we can make the pricing work."

[FALLBACK POSITION 1]
"Would you be able to offer me 50% off for the next 6 months while I evaluate whether I can continue the subscription at the full price? That would give me time to adjust my budget and see if I can make it work long-term."

[FALLBACK POSITION 2]
"Is there a temporary pause option or a lower-tier plan I could switch to? I'd rather downgrade than cancel completely. What options do you have for customers who want to stay but need to reduce costs?"

[FALLBACK POSITION 3]
"I've been looking at Disney+ and Hulu, and they're offering similar content for less. Would Netflix be willing to match a lower price point to keep me as a customer? I'd prefer to stay with Netflix since I'm familiar with the interface and my viewing history is here."

[OBJECTION HANDLING]
If they say "That's our best price":
"I appreciate that, but I've been a customer for 24 months and I'm hoping my loyalty counts for something. Is there a retention department or supervisor who might have more flexibility to work with long-term customers like me?"

If they say "We can't change pricing":
"I understand you may not have the authority to offer discounts. Could you transfer me to someone who does? I'd really like to explore all options before I have to cancel."

[CLOSING]
"I appreciate your time today. If you can check with your supervisor or retention team about any available discounts or offers, I'd be grateful. Can I expect a call back within 24-48 hours with some options? Otherwise, I may need to proceed with cancellation. Thank you for your help."
